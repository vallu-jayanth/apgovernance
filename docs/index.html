How we spot odd payments
Red flags
Paid more than the work's estimate or approved cost; the same amount paid twice for the same work; a voucher number used twice; paid in cash; a problem with the work's photo.
Worth asking
No photo of the work; no clear description; an amount just under a limit like ₹1 or ₹5 lakh (bills split to avoid checks); the same amount paid three or more times; paid on a Sunday.
Just so you know
Big round amounts; payments rushed in the last week of March.
Photo problems
No GPS; taken outside the state or far from the village; "finished" photo before the foundation photo ("finished before it started"); the same picture used for different works; many works photographed from one spot; and, where checked, a computer check that says the photo doesn't show the work, is blank, or is a photo of a screen. Computer checks can be wrong, so open the photo first.
Limits
The cash book has no bills or shop names. Matching a payment to a photo uses the work's name and can miss. Every flag is a question, not proof.
What you can do

Ask. It's your money.

1. Ask at the Gram Sabha

Show the flagged items. Ask to see the bills and the work.

2. File an RTI

Ask the panchayat office for copies of the bills and photos. They must reply in 30 days.

3. Go and look

Use the photo and map links. Is the work really there?

Questions to ask about the payments you loaded
What do these words mean?
Lakh / crore
1 lakh = ₹1,00,000. 1 crore = 100 lakh. 1 lakh crore = 1,00,000 crore.
Building new things
Roads, schools, buildings, water projects. Things that last.
Running costs
Salaries, pensions, interest on loans, subsidies, electricity.
Voucher
The record of one payment. A bill should be behind every one.
Receipts / bills
Papers showing what was bought and from whom.
CAG
India's official auditor. It checks how governments spend money.
